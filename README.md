Selected work (anonymized case studies)

What follows are the engagements I can still document end to end. They are samples from a much larger body of work — most of the rest ran on company systems and stayed behind when I left. The numbers are the real ones, from the records that survive.

Field Inventory & Billing — Tiger Bullets™ (Excel). I ran the tracking system for a
lost-circulation product program in an Arkansas shale play, 2009–2013. 55,886 sacks
(1.4M lbs) came in; 251 delivery tickets went out across 227 wells and 15 rigs;
$1.71M got invoiced, and every logged delivery closed with a signed ticket. One
workbook held deliveries, warehouse and field inventory, signed-ticket billing,
and the count set against the math — with the difference shown, not buried.
**Live dashboard (Tableau Public):** [Tiger Bullets Program, 2009–2013 — Field Inventory & Billing](https://public.tableau.com/views/TigerBulletsProgram20092013FieldInventoryBilling/Dashboard1)
Asset Register & Job-Cost Ledger. I kept the two records a multi-site oilfield
services company ran on: a register of 527 assets — serial numbers, warranties,
and a named location, work area, and person for every one, across five sites — and
a job-cost ledger of 5,116 purchased line items, $1.43M across 92 projects, every
line traceable from purchase order to vendor invoice to delivery ticket.
Asset, Compliance & Billing Database — MySQL + Python. The databases I built at
the office held what the company's Dynamics system didn't: rentals, billing, job
assets, certification and compliance dates, accounts receivable — 150+ assets
tracked across job sites. The reports management ran on came out of them, kept to
the penny. And when my numbers and the Dynamics general ledger didn't meet,
reconciling the two was my job.
